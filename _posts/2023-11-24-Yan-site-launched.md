Applications of Timber Structures and Wood Construction in Architecture
木结构与木构造在建筑中的应用


1. What is plywood?

The plywood used in timber construction is a type of laminated veneer lumber (LVL), specifically rotary-cut veneer laminated wood. The veneers are produced by mounting large debarked logs onto a rotating lathe and continuously peeling them into thin sheets. The veneer thickness typically ranges from 2.5 to 6.4 mm, and the length of each veneer is at least 150 times its thickness.

The rotary-cut veneers are then arranged in layers, bonded together with adhesive, and consolidated under heat and pressure to form laminated veneer lumber (LVL).

It should be noted that the number of veneer layers in LVL is generally an odd number, such as 3, 5, 7, or 9 plies, to maintain structural stability and achieve balanced construction.

2. What is a toothed connection (finger/tooth joint)?

A toothed connection is generally used in structural locations subjected to compressive forces. In this type of connection, a series of teeth are cut into the end of a compression member, with the teeth oriented perpendicular to the member axis. Matching grooves are then cut into the connecting member. The compressive force is transferred through the bearing surfaces between the teeth and the corresponding grooves.

The depth of the tooth groove is determined by structural calculations. In general, the maximum groove depth should not exceed one-third of the height of the bottom chord member.

When the axial force is relatively small, a single-tooth connection may be used. For larger axial forces, a double-tooth connection is adopted to provide greater load-carrying capacity.
